## Scope and statistical hypotheses

The purpose is to identify an independently falsifiable joint-model benefit, **not** to establish innovation as a substitute for predictive calibration. J1 is a constrained finite-pool shared-game model, J2 an uncertainty perturbation with some documented mean drift, J3 a fixed scenario mixture, and J4 a dependence-aware scoring/null experiment. Fitted real parameters and real prospective outcome validation are prerequisites, not generated by this prototype.

- **J1:** Common normal latent pace/script; each team's modeled plays are Poisson draws; conditional plays are allocated by a three-category multinomial into completed bookkeeping classes: called passes/attempts, sacks, rush plays. Player targets are mutually exclusive pass-attempt allocations (WR/TE and receiving RB, plus unassigned), catches are conditional on targets, receiving yards and touchdowns conditional on catches; RB/QB carries share a separate rush-play pool. The confirmed starting QB's attempts are a subset of team attempts. Defender solo/assists depend on opposing generated plays and defensive participation.
- **J2:** Retains source means and structural allocation but adds mean-one Gamma team-play variation (shape 25); one per-game random Gamma-normalized role-share vector (concentration 28); one per-game Beta catch-rate vector (effective sample 35); random defender snap rates; Student-t(5) conditional signed yardage; verified-only QB early-exit mixture. `P(Y)` is empirically sampled, not the output of an independent player-by-player analytic model. Even with mean-one upstream variation, normalized shares and rounded/truncated yards can move the marginal mean. This is a deliberate **uncertainty sensitivity**, not an isolated mathematically exact variance-only experiment.
- **J3:** Locked neutral 50%, pass-heavy underdog 20%, run-heavy favorite 20%, low tempo 10%. Each game draw samples a scenario once; scenario conditional distributions are sampled separately. Scenario effects require verifiable original pregame event/game-role receipts for any future source-certified attempt. These weights have **not** been fitted or adjusted to outcome data.
- **J4:** Audits QB attempts/receiver targets, QB completions/catches, RB carries/team pass attempts, opponent plays/defender tackles and competing receiver targets from the common joint samples. An independent-marginal null permutes each vector column without changing its empirical marginal distribution. Separate numerical functions compute empirical Energy Score, Variogram Score (default exponent 0.5), and pairwise exceedance co-occurrence. Without future legitimate multivariate observations, no predictive benefit is established.

For original line `L`, the same joint draw set yields `P(Over)=P(Y>L)`, `P(Under)=P(Y<L)`, and `P(Push)=P(Y=L)`, plus mean, variance, lower/median/upper quantiles, zero mass, count PMF when applicable and Monte Carlo standard error. Each event/model seed derives deterministically from immutable input identity, event ID, model ID and version. Version v0.2 adds receiving RBs to the same shared target/reception pool, preserving v0.1 output files as older experimental artifacts. No target-game actual or postgame feature is read by this simulator.

## Constrained accounting and explicit limitations

The model enforces its **internal modeled** play bookkeeping `plays = pass_attempts + sacks + rush_attempts`; starter attempts no larger than team attempts; player target sum no greater than team passes; all receptions no greater than targets; RB/QB credited carries no greater than team rush plays; `combined_tackles = solo_tackles + tackle_assists` for an individual. Counts are nonnegative integers. The defensive process uses at most one solo credit or two different assisted credits on a tackle event, with untracked-defender residual. Signed yardage is permitted. No independent over/under models violate complementarity.

These are **not complete official NFL accounting identities**. Penalty/no-play conditions, scrambles and sacks, special-teams tackles, official double-credit policies, substitutions, receiver eligibility, multiple passers, kneels, snaps, exact regulation/overtime exposure, and uncertain DNP/payout semantics are not faithfully modeled. Defensive share and opportunity-generation coefficients are synthetic, not learned. A sampled *physically consistent internal toy game* is not a calibrated NFL game predictor.
