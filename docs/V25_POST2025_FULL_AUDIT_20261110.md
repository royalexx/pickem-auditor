# Pick’em Auditor — full audit after the 2025 replay

November 10, 2026, America/Toronto. Audited model snapshot: `5c78653fd79ac2f9fe27d8385c1629b79fc126f8`; 2024 repair and 2025 frozen replay. Read-only audit implementation: [audit branch](https://github.com/royalexx/pickem-auditor/tree/chatgpt/v25-post2025-full-audit-20261110). Exact audit commit/PR are recorded in the evidence packet’s `GITHUB_HANDOFF.json`.

## Decision

**Targeted changes are needed. A “no changes needed” conclusion is not supported.** The repaired count model has demonstrated useful retrospective improvement, and its score arithmetic and chronological updates reproduce correctly. Several reliability, role, integration and validation issues remain. Keep this model as the research baseline while addressing the specific failures below. This audit does not change models, forecasts, source data, production, Tier rules, training or schedules.

The principal confidence comparison is **78.56% actual hits versus 83.04% average claimed confidence** across 905 selections at claimed probability ≥80%. Comparing the hit rate only with the 80% inclusion cutoff understates the deficit. The observed-minus-claimed difference is **-4.47 percentage points**. Descriptive 95% game-cluster interval: [-7.61, -1.58] pp; week-cluster interval: [-8.30, -0.98] pp. The selections span 255 games and 391 players, with 18 weekly clusters. These intervals remain retrospective and are not a prospective significance certificate after earlier 2025 exposure, repeated analysis and selection.

## What passed

- Independent frozen-file rescoring passed again for **both seasons**: 214,563 probability scores and 71,521 raw count scores for the 2024 repair; 198,591 probability scores and 66,197 count scores for 2025. Those are method/model score computations, not independent bets.
- All **288 saved calibration parameter maps** reproduce exactly from completed earlier weeks. An independent bounded optimizer found no material objective improvement for any of the **260 active global Platt maps**. There is no evidence here of a faulty Platt optimizer or hidden current-week labels in calibration.
- Exact event/team/player/stat settlement, saved line identities, empirical CRPS, original forecast hashes and weekly cutoffs reproduce. The 2024 input source and reports remain unchanged.
- All 18 saved 2025 origins have zero overlap between current scheduled event IDs and training event IDs. Across 2,718 historical source team-games, this audit found zero conflicting season/week/opponent groups and zero event-ID/year conflicts.
- The model beats stronger exploratory past-only controls on the same 35,566 expanding-model settlements:

| Binary probability method | Brier, lower is better |
|---|---:|
| Past market-only base rate | 0.2416 |
| Past role-and-line base rate | 0.2355 |
| Repaired raw model | 0.2309 |
| Role-calibrated model | 0.2303 |

Both controls use empirical stat-category outcome frequencies, not sportsbook odds. The role-and-line control uses earlier completed 2025 labels, Beta(1,1) smoothing, and a 30-observation fallback through role/market strata. It was added during this audit, so its comparison is exploratory, not preregistered. The role-calibrated model’s paired Brier improvement over it is -0.00525, with descriptive game-cluster interval [-0.00647, -0.00403]. These controls have no count distribution; the audit deliberately does not assign them the model’s CRPS or interval metrics.

- Final integration test suite: **1,389 tests ran, OK, nine skipped**; six new audit tests passed, including independent probability-law and optimizer checks. Passing tests do not certify every operational path.

## Finding 1 — current QB role is missing from a materially wrong prior

**Priority: high. Observed model error.** Sparse-history QBs are shrunk toward a broad positional population that is unsuitable for package/backup workload. In Week 3, Jalen Milroe’s pass-attempt forecast assigned **99.658% Over 0.5**. The saved input had one earlier count observation and the reconstructed count mean was **18.514 attempts**. The independent postgame snap source records **one offensive snap** and the source result was **zero attempts**. Jaxson Dart’s Week 3 Over 0.5 forecast similarly claimed 99.854%, followed by zero attempts on three offensive snaps.

The mean is not a numerical sampling error: with one observed zero and two prior pseudo-games, the role prior dominates. A QB position label does not establish starting, relief or package usage. These examples localize a structural source of excessive confidence, rather than blaming calibration alone.

Across 11 high-confidence QB selections covering attempts and completions, average claim was **89.58%**, actual hit **27.27%**. These props are correlated and the sample is small; the severity is an observed failure, not a precise general QB hit-rate estimate. Verified current role, projected offensive participation and change-state uncertainty are needed before confident QB publication.

## Finding 2 — aggregate confidence hides role-specific failure

**Priority: high. Observed reliability deficit.**

| Role | ≥80% selections | Average claim | Actual hit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| QB | 11 | 89.58% | 27.27% |
| LB | 122 | 82.61% | 69.67% |
| DL | 425 | 83.00% | 79.53% |
| DB | 173 | 83.20% | 82.66% |
| RB | 93 | 82.64% | 80.65% |
| WR | 51 | 82.03% | 80.39% |
| TE | 30 | 84.89% | 86.67% |

Linebackers are the major additional weakness: **69.67% actual versus 82.61% claimed on 122 selections**. Defensive backs are much closer: 82.66% versus 83.20%. A blanket confidence reduction would also change relatively well-behaved groups; fixes should be evaluated within meaningful workload/role cohorts. Tackle-assist selections separately hit 75.65% against an 83.28% claim on 382 rows.

The full-season high-confidence group remains 887 Unders and 18 Overs. Always selecting Under on those exact selected contracts would hit **78.78%**, versus the model’s 78.56%. The model’s high-confidence Overs hit 44.44% against an 86.50% claim. The 18-Over sample is small. This does not show that the model’s Under ranking is useless: the selected membership itself comes from the model. It does show that a high selected hit rate is insufficient evidence of balanced directional forecasting or sportsbook edge.

## Finding 3 — research and production still disagree on combined tackles

**Priority: high integration blocker. Verified in current `main` source reads.** The research replay and canonical source-stat challenger use:

`def_tackles_solo + def_tackles_with_assist + def_tackle_assists`.

The currently fetched production `fetch_slate._game_stat` path and `settle.py` fallback still use:

`def_tackles_solo + def_tackle_assists`.

The production blobs were verified read-only: collector `810cf3df5a5f0ac9c98095ad5d8250cce2d150b4`; settler `ae7cae3abf91031c75209c0a8993c2b319e07aa0`. The source-stat reconciliation module explicitly says it has not been integrated into production. This is therefore an existing unresolved integration issue, not a newly introduced replay arithmetic error.

Among 7,835 matched 2025 combined-tackle contracts, **519** have a nonzero omitted credit. At their saved synthetic thresholds, the two formulas yield different Over/Under outcomes in **94 cases**. None of the 60 ≥80% combined-tackle selections changes outcome under this formula-only comparison, so this does not explain away the current high-confidence deficit. No settlement was rewritten. Bookmaker/product stat semantics still require exact contract confirmation before production integration; a source-formula reconciliation is not that confirmation.

The collector also uses zero fallbacks for missing source components. Its raw source missingness behavior should be aligned with the strict unavailable/zero distinction. The frozen source files used in this replay do not exhibit the tested calendar conflicts.

## Finding 4 — confidence is conditional on a materially incomplete outcome cohort

**Priority: high validation limitation.** Of 1,271 archived expanding-model role-method offers at ≥80%, **905 settle and 366 are unmatched (28.80%)**. The displayed hit rate therefore does not validate all generated high-confidence candidates. Do not convert unmatched rows to losses, wins, zeros or DNPs without evidence.

The separate postgame snap audit matched 33,359 scored contracts to positive relevant offensive/defensive snaps. It found **2,162 with observed zero relevant snaps**, nine unmatched snap records and 36 missing ID crosswalks. In the ≥80% group, **52** have zero relevant snaps, **851** positive relevant snaps and two unavailable crosswalks. Positive special-teams snaps can coexist with zero offensive/defensive exposure. These are diagnostic categories, not automatically sportsbook void decisions, and cannot be used as hindsight pregame filters.

All 2025 lines remain synthetic. The prior-median-plus-half rule, unrestricted historical candidate pool and absent real offers can affect both Under concentration and apparent reliability. No production 60/40 or compatible timestamped market comparator was recovered for these contracts. Economic edge remains unmeasured.

## Finding 5 — small but exact inconsistencies between count marginals

**Priority: medium mathematical defect.** Receptions cannot exceed targets. Therefore at every common cutoff k, P(receptions > k) must be no greater than P(targets > k). The same first-order constraint holds between solo tackles/assists and their combined total.

Independent Poisson/negative-binomial marginals sometimes violate these necessary CDF inequalities. Using reconstructed analytic laws, not noisy sample comparisons, **43 of 28,689 candidate-pair checks** have a violation exceeding one percentage point: 32 reception/target pairs, ten solo/combined pairs and one assist/combined pair. The largest is **5.14 percentage points**. No completion/attempt pair exceeded the one-point threshold in this audit.

The comparisons scan integer cutoffs 0–80, so smaller or more extreme-tail violations may also exist. These are rare and do not explain the entire directional deficit. They still prevent treating the marginal family as fully coherent. The earlier rejected joint allocator should remain rejected; simple constrained marginals or conditional submodels can be tested against the same count baseline without assuming a large joint model is better.

## Finding 6 — Monte Carlo noise changes which selections clear 80%

**Priority: medium numerical/reporting defect.** The accepted marginal laws are Poisson or negative-binomial, so their individual line probabilities can be computed analytically. The replay instead estimates them using 2,048 samples.

Against the reconstructed analytic law, raw probability RMSE is **1.056 percentage points**. Holding the saved calibrators fixed, analytic probabilities change ≥80% membership for **169 settled contracts**. Of the 905 originally selected, 97 leave the cohort and 72 enter; the analytic cohort contains 880. Thus 10.7% of the original high-confidence membership depends on sampling near the threshold.

This does not prove the analytic pipeline improves forecast skill; calibration would need consistent chronological refitting and validation if its input probabilities change. It identifies avoidable numerical variation. Analytic marginal probabilities can coexist with retained Monte Carlo samples for distribution diagnostics and future joint research.

## Finding 7 — two adversarial input validation gaps remain

**Priority: high before release. Robustness defects; no observed leakage in the saved run.**

1. The forecaster accepts a historical record with the current fixture’s exact event ID if its season/week fields are relabeled as past. It checks calendar fields but does not independently reject current fixture identity in training.
2. The raw normalizer accepts player rows for one event/team that disagree on season, week and opponent. The first row silently supplies the team-game metadata.

Executable canaries demonstrate both acceptances. All actual saved source groups and weekly inputs checked here are consistent. Therefore the audit found **a validation boundary weakness, not proof that the completed replay leaked future labels**. Add hard identity/calendar/source-consistency checks and adversarial regressions before any release. Historical public availability remains separately uncertified.

## Finding 8 — warmup and forecast scope need clearer reporting

**Priority: medium.** Thirty-two of the 905 “role-method” high-confidence selections were actually raw identity outputs because the calibration map was inactive. The full saved maps expose this, but rows and summary labels do not explicitly distinguish warmup from fitted calibration. The truly fitted subset still has a confidence deficit: **78.58% actual versus 82.98% claimed on 873 selections**. Relabeling warmup alone will not repair the result.

The eight-market claim also does not mean every player/stat combination is modeled. QB carries are retained in normalized history but are omitted by the QB forecast group. The raw 2025 source has **664 QB player-games with carry fields, 584 with nonzero carries**. FB carries/targets and WR carries are also excluded. Those rows are not evidence that real offers existed, but supported quote contracts for these combinations must not disappear silently when this research interface is integrated into slate evaluation.

## Prioritized repair plan

1. **Unify exact stat definitions and harden event/calendar consistency.** Keep existing archives immutable; reconcile source and product semantics explicitly and add tests for each rejecting boundary. Re-run affected research comparisons separately if definitions change.
2. **Repair current role and workload priors, starting with QB packages/backups and linebackers.** Capture verified pregame role/participation evidence and model uncertainty in transitions. Preserve observed zero versus missing distinctions; never select by realized postgame snaps.
3. **Compute exact marginal line probabilities and enforce necessary CDF coherence.** Retain sample arrays and compare the complete chronological probability/calibration pipeline to the frozen baseline. Do not force equal Over/Under selection rates.
4. **Expose calibration-active status, quote eligibility and missing-outcome coverage per row.** Preserve all candidate and abstention reasons. Address QB rushing scope when source-supported contracts exist.
5. **Use prospective, source-certified real-offer evidence for acceptance.** 2024 and 2025 have informed development. Retain their frozen results as diagnostic references and avoid repeated tuning presented as untouched validation.

No audit result justifies changing NUTS convergence thresholds, restarting historical training, replacing the valid Platt optimizer, restoring high-confidence tiers or promoting the model. The existing simpler count baseline and its score implementation remain useful controls.

## Evidence, methods and reproducibility

The evidence packet contains both season re-verification receipts, complete audit metrics, all calibration refit/optimality results, all analytic CDF checks, chronological baseline scores, snap source/crosswalk and strict joins, validation canaries, preservation hashes and test logs. The original 2025 and 2024 full replay packets remain separate and unchanged. Audit scripts only read those packets and create new outputs.

During audit development, a preliminary binary baseline result inherited count-metric fields from a paired row. That audit-only reporting defect was corrected and the diagnostic was rerun before conclusions were finalized. The final baseline metrics contain no count CRPS/mean claims. Preliminary audit code/results are preserved and explicitly separated from `diagnostics_final`; none changed model forecasts or the proper-score conclusions.

The independent calibration check reproduces saved chronological maps and cross-checks the active global regularized objective with bounded L-BFGS-B. Role offsets reproduce exactly through the original prior-only routine; a separate joint optimizer for all role offsets was not claimed. Sources were retrospectively retrieved, and snap counts are postgame diagnostics only. Cluster intervals have only 18 weekly units and do not correct prior model selection or repeated testing. All newly introduced baseline/role analyses are exploratory.
