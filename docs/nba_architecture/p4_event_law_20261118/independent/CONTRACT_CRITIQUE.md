## Clock and score objections

- A defensive rebound implies control at its timestamp. If the next possession starts later, account for that live possession time explicitly. A turnover or scoring event can end the current possession before the next live inbound, but elapsed game clock cannot advance during a dead-ball wait. An explicit inbound/terminal-control ordering or bounded same-time convention is required.
- Specify exact event offsets, ordering at equal clocks, clock-side assignment at substitutions, rim contact before a shot-clock reset and period boundary behavior. Assists and shooter credits share the made-basket event. A live rebound may use a later eligible lineup only if an intervening dead-ball stoppage actually permits the change.
- P3's integrated schedule witnesses alone provide no dead-ball substitution evidence. Arbitrary mid-control transitions must fail, or a bounded explicit stoppage mechanism must be specified and witnessed. Substitutions cannot be added to a live rebound to force alignment. Fractional endpoints must not be rounded into alignment. Sum caps say nothing about the actual clock of an injury exit.
- A missing compatible native schedule is an interface refusal with a complete denominator. A controlled fixture with explicit legal rotation stops can establish a narrower engineering claim. Its conditions, parameters and comparison limits must be fixed before a study uses its outputs.
