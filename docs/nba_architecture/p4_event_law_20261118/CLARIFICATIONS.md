# Prespecified execution amendments

Read together with the unchanged preregistration at 367dd99. These amendments precede candidate execution.

- Live control transfers at the slot boundary, where the terminal event takes place. The terminal event uses the left-limit lineup, then substitutions take effect before the next slot starts. At a horn the terminal release and resolution are ordered immediately before expiration, using a left-limit symbolic clock. Tick timestamps denote nominal elapsed seconds, with this explicit order. The first event in a slot occurs at end minus four seconds. If it terminates the slot, place it at the terminal instant instead. For retained sequences the first resolution and any penalty/trip share the initial action clock with ordered subevents. The continuation uses the terminal instant. At most one offensive rebound continuation is allowed. A retained penalty's continuation may itself have one offensive rebound and a second shot, so the penalty is at end minus four, the retained miss/rebound at end minus two, and the terminal shot at the boundary. Made baskets, defensive recoveries and turnovers transfer control at the terminal instant.
- Exact O lineup changes are treated as exogenous stoppages. At an interior stint boundary the law inserts a retained out-of-bounds stoppage during live control with no box-score event or possession transfer. At a simultaneous terminal clock it uses the existing dead-ball substitution abstraction, after any resolution. A terminal live defensive rebound and simultaneous substitution are ordered with a retained out-of-bounds stoppage for the new offense before its next live action. A clock tie with an interior shot/FT action applies substitutions first. This is an engineering construction conditioned on O's clock, with stopped wall-clock time omitted. Actual dead-ball timestamps and real exit timestamps have no admission path. The slot timing and a continuation of at most four live seconds keep the constructed events inside the original or reset shot clock.
- A fixed-latent conditional law and finite execution law are distinct. Among completed runs the prior on latents is tilted by completion probability `1−(1−Z)^128` in each scenario, and by the product across independently seeded scenarios for a complete packet. In the forward process described here `Z` remains a function of the fixed latent parameters and schedule. The native study reports attempts, per-checkpoint failures and the observed completion rate. Exact finite checks report analytic normalizers for their specified subcases. Without a computed or bounded native Z, latent tilt and native rare-tail equivalence remain unresolved.
- For KS against NB, evaluate integer thresholds through the maximum physically possible count, then take the limit. Beyond that bound the empirical CDF is one and the exact source survival function is decreasing. Comparison uses survival functions directly at rare cutoffs. Include the fixed weighted sum of sampled O states and report positive-exposure membership separately. One failed seed prevents study-level PMF publication, leaving the complete raw paths available for diagnosis.
- Distributional adequacy requires intervals to clear the preregistered limits; with 256 clusters, wide bounds mean inconclusive even if estimates match. An estimate whose lower error bound exceeds its tolerance fails. This study is designed for screening. The combo comparison has no legacy acceptance target.
