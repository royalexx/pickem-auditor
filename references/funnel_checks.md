### Named funnel checks

Run every check for each sport on the slate. Pace, opponent environment and role
are scouting categories for Engine B. They never multiply Engine A's historical mean.
Several descriptions of one causal event count once. Two pace checks are still one
channel. A second channel is a different fact, not a second copy of pace.
Use a covered stat and cite how the event reaches this player's opportunities.
Missing evidence is not a neutral finding. Record it as not found and abstain where
needed. `funnel_channels` is legacy metadata, not a numerical context weight.


#### NFL funnel checks

- **Run funnel.** Opponent environment. Elite pass-coverage metrics and poor rushing EPA allowed, forcing this offense into heavy run volume. Moves rush attempts, rushing yards, and tackles + assists for a back or a box defender this actually reaches. Search: `"[Opponent] pass coverage metric"` and `"[Opponent] rushing EPA allowed"`.
- **Pass funnel.** Opponent environment. Elite rush defense and high EPA per dropback allowed, forcing this offense off the ground. Moves pass attempts, passing yards, completions, receptions, and receiving yards. Search: `"[Opponent] rush defense EPA"` and `"[Opponent] EPA per dropback allowed"`.
- **Heavy rush volume vs inside linebackers.** Opponent environment. An offense with 30 or more designed runs per game raises tackle-and-assist chances for a box linebacker who stays on the field. The linebacker's snap share is already the NFL scheme search. Search only the run volume: `"[Opponent] designed runs per game"`. If the defender leaves on passing downs, the G2 tackles veto still wins.
- **Shadow corner.** Opponent environment. An elite corner tracking the WR1, with targets moving to the WR2, WR3, or tight end. Moves receptions and receiving yards for the receiver who actually gets the targets, and can cut the WR1. Search: `"[Corner] shadow [WR1] [date]"`.
- **Nickel and slot.** Opponent environment. High target share and completion rate to the slot while the outside is locked down. Moves receptions, receiving yards, and completions for the slot. Search: `"[Opponent] slot target share"` and `"[Opponent] slot completion rate"`.
- **Blitz rate.** Opponent environment. A high opponent blitz rate means quick screens, dump-offs, and shallow targets to running backs and tight ends. Moves receptions, receiving yards, and completions for those players. Search: `"[Opponent] blitz rate"`.
- **Two-high and Cover 2.** Opponent environment. Underneath volume, checkdowns, and short catches for secondary receivers. Use the existing `"two-high safety rate [Opponent]"` search. Do not run a second query.
- **Cover 1 and Cover 0.** Opponent environment. Man coverage with no deep safety help, boundary one-on-one shots for a perimeter wideout. Moves receptions, receiving yards, and passing yards when this receiver is the one in the one-on-one. Search: `"[Opponent] Cover 1 rate"` and `"[Opponent] Cover 0 rate"`.
- **Pace and play count.** Pace. A no-huddle opponent raises snap counts for both teams. Moves every NFL counting stat this player's snaps, routes, or carries actually track. Search: `"[Opponent] plays per game"` and `"[Opponent] no-huddle rate"`. Do not count pace twice in the scenario opportunity estimate.
- **Spread and game script.** Opponent environment. Large underdogs can mean late passing and garbage-time receptions. Heavy favorites can mean second-half clock-killing carries. Use the spread and total search. G5 still rejects an NFL rushing Over for a back on a team that is an underdog of 6.5 or more, and an NBA starter Over at a spread of 9.5 or larger. `SCRIPT_RISK` still covers 4.5 up to but not including 6.5, and 8.0 up to but not including 9.5. Do not add a new spread line.
- **Vacated targets and carries.** Role. A starting skill player is out and the usage moves to the direct backup. Use the "Who inherits the work" search. Use sourced upcoming-role scenarios in Engine B. Historical role windows in Engine A require actual games.
- **Backup or rookie quarterback.** Opponent environment. A backup or rookie starter means short-area targets and safety valves to running backs. Use the injury report and lineup searches, which already ask for quarterback status. Moves receptions and receiving yards for the back who actually gets those targets. Pass props for a player who is not the expected starting quarterback stay the G2 veto.
- **Pass rush against the offensive line.** Opponent environment. A front that beats this line forces quick releases, swing passes, slants, and screens. Own and opponent injury searches already ask for offensive-line and pass-rush absences. The rate is the pass-rush win rate search on the next check. Moves receptions and receiving yards for the screen and slant options.
- **Pass-rush win rate.** Opponent environment. A high opponent pass-rush win rate means rollouts, scramble yards, and designed rushes for a mobile quarterback. Moves rushing yards and rush attempts for that quarterback. Search: `"[Opponent] pass rush win rate"`.
- **Severe weather.** Opponent environment. Sustained wind and freezing rain move the call sheet from deep passing to the ground game and box tackles. Use the weather search. Wind uses `WEATHER_RISK` from 15.0 mph up to but not including 18.0, and the G5 hard veto at 18 mph, including outdoor NHL shots-on-goal Overs. Do not add a 20 mph line. Snow, freezing rain, and other precipitation stay the G5 and `WEATHER_RISK` intensity rules. A ground-game or tackles side that the weather supports is SUPPORT. It does not get its own probability bump.
- **Tight end over the middle.** Opponent environment. Linebacker and safety units that allow intermediate targets to tight ends. Moves receptions and receiving yards for the tight end who runs those routes. Search: `"[Opponent] tight end targets allowed"`.
- **Soft interior.** Opponent environment. Light defensive tackles and between-the-tackles carries for the early-down back. Moves rush attempts and rushing yards for that back. Search: `"[Opponent] interior run defense"`.
- **Corner room depletion.** Opponent environment. Boundary-corner injuries and targets at the backup corner. Use the opponent injury report and lineup search. Moves receptions and receiving yards for the receiver who faces the backup.
- **Red-zone personnel.** Role. 12 or 22 personnel inside the 10, goal-line touches for a power back or a short-yardage tight end. Grade only a covered stat: rush attempts, rushing yards, receptions, or receiving yards. Do not grade a touchdown. Search: `"[Team] red zone personnel"`.
- **Man-coverage scramble.** Opponent environment. Defensive backs turned away from the line, scramble yards for a mobile quarterback. Moves rushing yards for that quarterback. Search: `"[QB] man coverage scramble rate"`.

#### NBA funnel checks

- **Vacated usage.** Role. A primary scorer or handler is out and shot volume moves to the next option. Use the "Who inherits the work" search. Model the upcoming change in Engine B scenarios. Historical role-change windows belong to Engine A only after the games have occurred.
- **Pace-up.** Pace. A fast opponent raises possessions and attempt baselines. Use the existing `"[Team] pace possessions"` search. Do not count pace twice when scenario opportunities already incorporate it. The cited pace can still be one mechanism. Engine A receives no pace multiplier.
- **Drop coverage.** Opponent environment. A shot blocker in drop funnels pull-up midrange, floaters, and pick-and-pop threes. Use the existing `"[Opponent] drop coverage"` search. Moves points and threes for the player who takes those shots.
- **Corner threes.** Opponent environment. A defense that collapses and kicks out to catch-and-shoot wings. Moves threes for the wing who spots up. Search: `"[Opponent] corner threes allowed"`.
- **Defensive rebounding.** Opponent environment. A poor defensive-rebound rate means offensive rebounds and putbacks for the opposing frontcourt player who actually crashes. Moves rebounds and points. Search: `"[Opponent] defensive rebound rate"`.
- **Foul pressure.** Opponent environment. A foul-prone defense and free throws inside a points prop for a driver. Free throws are not their own market. Moves points, PRA, PR, PA, or RA only. Search: `"[Opponent] fouls per game"` and `"[Player] free throw rate"`.
- **Pick-and-roll switch.** Opponent environment. An isolation player hunting a weak defender. Moves points, assists, or threes for that initiator. Search: `"[Opponent] pick and roll switch rate"`.
- **Back-to-back rest.** Role. A star sitting, minutes moving to a reserve. Use the rest search. `REST_SPOT` still flags an Over on a back-to-back or a third game in four nights. Do not add a second rest flag.
- **Paint points.** Opponent environment. Poor point-of-attack defense and rim touches for a center or slasher who actually gets them. Moves points. Search: `"[Opponent] paint points allowed"`.
- **Trap on the point guard.** Opponent environment. A trap leaves a short-roll assist chance for the rolling big. Moves assists for that big. Search: `"[Opponent] point guard trap"`.
- **Live-ball turnovers.** Opponent environment. Transition points for wings. Steals are not a graded market. Moves points for the wing in the open floor. Search: `"[Opponent] live ball turnovers"`.
- **Opponent three-point volume.** Opponent environment. A high three-point attempt rate leaves long rebounds for guards and wings who actually pursue them. Moves rebounds. Search: `"[Opponent] three point attempt rate"`.
- **Undersized frontcourt.** Opponent environment. No rim protector, post touches and rebounds for a traditional center. Use the opponent lineup search. C1 already asks for the starting center or rim protector.
- **Blowout spread.** Role. Early starter benching and fourth-quarter minutes for depth. Use the spread search and the existing `"[Coach] rotation minutes"` search. G5 still rejects NBA starter Overs at a spread of 9.5 or larger. `SCRIPT_RISK` still covers 8.0 up to but not including 9.5. Do not add a new spread line.
- **Assists allowed.** Opponent environment. Ball movement and assist chances for the primary handler. Moves assists. Search: `"[Opponent] assists allowed"`.
- **Shots blocked.** Opponent environment. Contested layups and block chances for an anchor center. Blocks are not an NBA covered market. Use this only when the cited rate changes points, rebounds, or threes for a covered prop. Otherwise record "not a graded market". Search: `"[Opponent] blocks allowed"`.
- **Five-out spacing.** Opponent environment. A perimeter center pulls the rim protector out and opens cutting lanes for an off-ball guard. Moves points and assists for that guard. Search: `"[Team] five-out spacing"`.
- **Road-trip fatigue.** Opponent environment. A team missing perimeter shots, defensive rebounds for the frontcourt that actually boards. The rest search covers back-to-backs and short weeks only. Search the trip: `"[Team] road trip [date]"`. Do not invent a road-trip flag. If the spot is also a back-to-back, `REST_SPOT` still applies to an Over.

#### NHL funnel checks

- **Opponent penalty minutes.** Opponent environment. High penalty minutes mean power-play time and shots for the PP1 unit that is actually on the ice. PP1 personnel is already the `"[Team] PP1"` search. Search the rate: `"[Opponent] penalty minutes per game"`. Moves shots on goal. Do not grade power-play points.
- **Shot volume.** Pace. Shot attempts and shots against per 60 in a high-event game. Use the existing `"[Opponent] shots against per 60"` search. Corsi is this rate, not a separate name. Do not search a second brand for the same number. Moves shots on goal for a skater who faces that pace.
- **Shot suppression.** Opponent environment. A team allowing a high shots-against rate means save chances for the opposing goalie. Use the same shots-against search. Moves goalie saves. Use a supported opponent sample and state its uncertainty in Engine B.
- **Perimeter shots.** Opponent environment. The defense protects the slot and leaves point shots for opposing defensemen who actually play the point. Moves shots on goal for those defensemen. Search: `"[Opponent] point shot rate"`.
- **Backup or call-up goalie.** Role. A confirmed backup starter can mean more shots from the opposing lines. Use the `"[Goalie] confirmed starter"` search. Saves with an unconfirmed starter stay the G2 hard veto. An unconfirmed starter on a shots prop stays `UNCONFIRMED_GOALIE`. Do not add a third goalie rule.
- **Top-line injury.** Role. A top-six forward is out and a lower-line forward moves onto a line with a playmaker. Use the "Who inherits the work" search. Role-change rules apply. Do not pass a rate ratio for the same ice-time jump the new-role window already shows.
- **Home last change.** Opponent environment. The home coach can put a top line on a weak pair or a bottom line. Moves shots on goal for the line that gets the matchup. Search: `"[Home team] last change [date]"`.
- **Offensive-zone starts.** Role. A line with a cited high offensive-zone start share and immediate shots. Moves shots on goal for a forward on that line. Search: `"[Player] offensive zone start rate"`.
- **High-event rushes.** Pace. Odd-man rushes and transition shots for attacking forwards. The shots-against search is the volume. This check is the rush chance. Search: `"[Team] odd man rush rate"`. Moves shots on goal.
- **Penalty-kill ice.** Role. A high-volume opponent power play means blocked shots for penalty-killing defensemen who actually kill. Use the opponent penalty-minutes search for the volume. Search the workload: `"[Player] penalty kill time on ice"`. Moves blocked shots.
- **Point-shot blocks.** Opponent environment. Opponent point shots mean blocked shots for checkers and penalty killers who actually stand in that lane. Use the point-shot search above. Do not run a second query. Moves blocked shots.
- **Back-to-back travel.** Pace. The second game of a back-to-back and shots against that goalie. Use the rest search. `REST_SPOT` still flags an Over in a back-to-back. Do not add a travel threshold.
- **Trailing script.** Opponent environment. Late 6-on-5 shot volume. Do not grade goals, points, or empty-net points. Shots on goal are the covered stat. Search: `"[Team] 6 on 5 shot rate"`. The spread search can say whether a trail is likely. It does not add a spread veto for NHL.
- **Soft penalty kill.** Opponent environment. A weak kill and shot volume for the opposing power play. Do not grade points. Moves shots on goal for the PP1 player who is on the ice. Search: `"[Opponent] penalty kill rate"`.
- **Dump-and-chase.** Opponent environment. Board play can support a blocked-shot prop for a defender who blocks from the wall. Hits are not a graded market. Search: `"[Team] dump and chase rate"`.
- **Faceoff mismatch.** Opponent environment. A strong center against a weak one can support shots after wins for a linemate who stays on the ice. Faceoff wins are not a graded market. Search: `"[Center] faceoff win rate vs [opponent]"`.
- **Blue-line injury.** Role. A top-pair defenseman is out and minutes move to the partner who remains. Use the "Who inherits the work" search. Role-change rules apply.
- **Neutral-zone turnovers.** Opponent environment. Giveaways and rush shots for transition wingers who actually lead the rush. Moves shots on goal. Search: `"[Opponent] neutral zone giveaways"`.
